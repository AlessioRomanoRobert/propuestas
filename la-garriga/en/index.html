01 — Story

Seventy years
of bocadillos

Two Catalan partners opened a delicatessen in Santa Cruz and called it La Garriga. Half the island can tell you what happened next.

Directo al Paladar ↗Diario de Avisos ↗

  1. 1950s

    A Catalan charcutería

    Two partners from Catalonia set up La Garriga in central Santa Cruz: serrano ham, chorizo, cheeses and cold cuts sliced to order.

  2. 1953

    Nothing goes to waste

    They started turning the trimmings left over from slicing into a tortilla. Thrifty cooking that became, as Directo al Paladar puts it, the goose that laid the golden eggs.

  3. The recipe

    Chopped cured meats, lots of egg

    Today the mix is chopped on purpose: pork loin, ham, chorizo, salchichón and cooked ham, browned in sunflower oil, bound with beaten egg and flipped in the pan. And crisp bread: the owner's tip is to keep it in the fridge.

  4. Today

    900 to 1,000 a day

    That's the daily pace, according to the house; on Three Kings' Day it can top 4,000. Still on Calle Pérez Galdós, now at number 19.

The people behind the bar

In 2022, Diario de Avisos said goodbye to Lorenzo and José Manuel, two of the makers of these sandwiches, as they retired. Their colleagues put it like this:

“We're losing a friend, a father, a colleague, a confidant and a fine professional.” (translated)

Source: Diario de Avisos, 27/04/2022 ↗
